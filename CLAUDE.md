# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Project

"Federal Monkey Patrol" (codename Kong): a 2004–2006 game by Dave Lawrence (code) and Sam Norman (art), GPL v2. You fly a biplane over a procedurally generated city shooting monkeys; `game.txt` holds the design notes and `todo.html` the author's TODO/refactoring ideas.

The main version is now a browser port (Vite + Three.js, TypeScript) deployed to GitHub Pages. The original C++ / OpenGL / SDL 1.2 code is in `legacy/` for history only (tag `v2006-native` has the pre-port tree): the port no longer tracks it, and the code shouldn't point back to it or keep C++ behaviour just for compatibility. Milestone 1 (issue #1) is a faithful recreation of what the 2006 build actually did: no new gameplay. Things that were switched off or never implemented in 2006 (Giant Claw meter, skip key, small monkeys) stay off until later milestones. Since milestone 1, the user has asked for some additions: a throttle (the plane had no forward velocity in 2006), top-down flying (first a switch, now its own game modes), on-screen touch controls, more responsive controls with a chase camera, a procedurally textured 1920s–40s New York (midrise buildings, facades, street furniture, period street markings), top-down mode climbing clear of the roofs, a friendlier story (stacked dialogue, slower timing in `story.properties`, tap to advance, skip link, pause menu), an old-movie look (colour, but graded most of the way to black and white), billboard trees (now fixed crossed quads), level of detail for the city, a spinning propeller, collisions (the plane explodes on hitting a building or the street and comes back over the rooftops, blinking and briefly invulnerable; `lives` in `game.properties`, shown as Harlow's heads, then GAME OVER and status `LOST`; bullets throw sparks, bombs explode; synthesised sounds for the guns, bullets hitting stone or kongs and bombs, fading with distance from the camera), kongs (King Kong-sized apes, `kong2.obj`, rigged and animated procedurally (walk, climb, chest-beating roar, fall; `kongtest.html` shows the clips): they walk the streets, sometimes climb a building to roar from its roof, and fall after 5–10 hits, counting `MONKEY_DEATHS` (one shot up on a building tumbles off it, bouncing down any ledges, steered out over the middle of the street and down to it, never left lying on a roof); tuned in `kong.properties`; `on="rooftops"` puts them on flat spots near the tops of the tall skyscrapers (within `TOWER_TOP` of the highest place to stand on towers of `TOWER_MIN` and up; found by sweeping down; with none free a kong waits) where they stay, turning and roaring, `<kongs>N</kongs>` in a mission's `<startconditions>` sets how many), a patrol area (a circle of `patrol_radius` round the start, in `game.properties`: the kongs keep inside it, a heading-up radar at the top right shows them and its edge (and in missions with a fixed number of kongs, an arrow under the tally, `KongPointer`, points to the nearest within `pointer_range`: where it is on screen, or BEHIND), and flying out gives `turn_back_seconds`, with the clock stopped while heading back in, before losing a plane and being turned round to face the middle), a "Last updated" line on the menu (build time and commit, from `define` in `vite.config.ts`), a minimum speed that goes by altitude (the throttle's range in `game.properties` runs from `*_low` at `speed_low_height` to `*_high` at `speed_high_height`, slower down among the streets, faster high up; top-down mode never goes below the min either), game modes picked on the title screen (each a `.game` file holding one mission: Clear the Area (`clear.xml`, a fixed number of kongs, won when `MONKEY_POPULATION` reaches 0), Monkey Mayhem (`mayhem.xml`, no patrol area or radar: 40 weaker kongs kept within `roam_radius` of a point ahead of the plane wherever it flies, those left behind replaced at once; three minutes), Rooftops and Rooftops: Steering (`rooftops.xml`, `rooftops_steer.xml`: like Mayhem, but top-down with every kong on a roof, twice the usual size; the camera is close over the plane (`TOPDOWN_CAMERA`) so it shows big, and the plane flies high over the roofs (`TOPDOWN_CLEARANCE` in `mission.ts`) so the city still shows wide; no throttle and a fixed speed range (`topdown_min_speed`/`topdown_max_speed`), up/down on the stick changes speed; the plane keeps a kong's height more clear of the roofs; 1942 controls keep the heading and slide the plane at 80% speed, the plane leaning off the middle of the screen; steering controls turn it in a `STEER_RADIUS` circle, round a block, at any speed) and Free Patrol (`fly.game`); missions will later be slotted into the story; an outcome's message shows as a banner with how it went (on a success only how it went; the message is on the menu)), top-down bullets that make that mode 2D (they fly level over the city, hit whatever their path crosses on the map at any height, and curve down onto it), and blondes (the 2006 `blonde.obj`, rigged and animated procedurally like the kongs: a climbing kong may stop a few storeys up, reach in at a window and pull one out, then climb one-handed on up the setbacks to the top of a tall tower, roar there holding her up, and later climb down and carry her on; shot, it lets go and she floats down slowly, steered out to the middle of the street (bounced off any ledges on the way), for the plane to fly through and catch (anywhere along its path that frame), `BLONDE_RESCUES`, with a cartoon heart or lipstick kiss popping up on screen (`KissPop`), or lands, `BLONDE_CASUALTIES`; a marker over her and a radar blip (both a symbol of blondness in one golden line, no fill: a circle for the head inside the outline of a 1930s bob with a side parting, no face, `drawBlondeHead()` in `hud.ts`), and her head "x2" under the kong tally (a kong's head rendered once from the model, `KongTroop.portrait()`, "x3", `IconTally`); not in the rooftop modes; tuned in `blonde.properties`), a link to the animation test page from the menu's extras, the story as mission, story, mission, story... (`data/federal_monkey_patrol.game`: it starts straight in, with a short film leader countdown, 2, 1, then each mission's title card, up just long enough to read: Kill 3 Kongs (`killmonkeys.xml`, three kongs, `<blondes>off</blondes>`), Survive (`survive1.xml`: top-down, 1942 controls, kongs on the roofs throwing bananas, a minute), Save 3 Blondes (`saveblondes.xml`: Mayhem with blondes, no time limit), Survive Again (`survive2.xml`: steering, more kongs throwing faster), Rescue 3 Blondes, Lose None (`rescue.xml`: the chase camera, blondes everywhere, one landing loses the game); between them short flashback intermissions (`data/story/intermissions/*.shot`, cut down from the 2006 cutscenes to the one-liners: Harlow woken in his basement, the signature and "Get the man his plane!", back at base, the party plane and his underpants; the Giant Claw is cut), ending with Harlow flying the blondes and a crate of whiskey off to recuperate and, heard over the radio on black, going down again, then The End; the menu's "The Story: flow only" (`?flow=1`, `GameManager.fromFile(..., { flow })`) swaps each mission for a `MissionCard` naming it and quoting its XML's top comment, to check the order without playing, and every title card waits for a tap (`holdForTap`, `TapWatch` in `input.ts`); the flashbacks before the first flight (stories 1 and 2) are labelled with a `caption` line in the `.shot` ("Earlier that morning...", "Earlier..."), a sepia plate on the picture; every title card and the leader are sepia with slow grain (`SEPIA_FILM` in `simple.ts`, grain and flicker from `film.ts`)), bananas (`bananas.ts`, `<bananas every_msecs rise_secs range hit_radius>` in `<startconditions>`: each kong in range and on screen throws one soon after it comes on screen and then every `every_msecs`, endlessly, in an arc whose top is at the plane's height where the plane will be after `rise_secs`; in top-down missions they're 2D like the bullets, hitting near the top of the arc whatever the height; a hit crashes the plane), and a game-over shot (`finale.ts`: when the last plane crashes the camera flies to a kong on a roof (or one put on a tall building behind the plane), closing in as it beats its chest and roars, buildings in the way cut out of the picture (`seethrough.ts`), then fades to a GAME OVER title card).


## Build & run

- `npm install`, then `npm run dev` (Vite dev server) or `npm run build` (type-checks, then builds the static site in `dist/`). `npm run typecheck` runs `tsc` alone; Vite itself only strips types, so the dev server runs code with type errors.
- TypeScript is strict (`tsconfig.json` for `src/`, `tsconfig.node.json` for `vite.config.ts`), `noEmit`, and local imports use the `.ts` extension. Enum-likes are `as const` objects with a type of the same name (`Status`, `Button`, `Stat`).
- The game fetches its original data files at runtime, with paths relative to the site root (the repo root): `data/`, and the `*.game` files at the root. The build copies them into `dist/` as-is (see `vite.config.ts`); they don't go through Vite's asset pipeline.
- `kongtest.html` (`src/kongtest.ts`, also built and deployed, linked from the menu's extras) is a test page for the kong's and blonde's animations: the clips on a moving ground and a sliding wall, the kong carrying a blonde, with the skeleton and skin weights (lil-gui), plus URL parameters for a contact sheet of a cycle (`?sheet=walk&views=side,front`, `?model=blonde&sheet=held`, `?carry=1&sheet=roar&zoom=4`), handy for checking poses in a headless browser.
- `?game=<path>` picks the `.game` file (default: `default_game` in `data/etc/main.properties`). The page always starts at a menu, because browsers need a click before audio can play.
- Deploy: `.github/workflows/pages.yml` builds and deploys to Pages on every push to `main`.
- There are no unit tests beyond the type check; check changes by running the game, headless with the harness:
  - `npm run shoot -- <page> [<page> ...]` (`tools/shoot.mjs`) starts the Vite dev server, opens each page in headless Chromium (playwright-core's chrome-headless-shell from `~/.cache/ms-playwright`, software WebGL), waits for it to finish, saves a full-page screenshot in `shots/` (git-ignored) and prints the page's console. It exits non-zero on a page error, a timeout or a failed check. Options: `--out`, `--timeout`, `--wait` (for pages that never say they're done), `--size`, `--tap` (with `--every`: tap the middle of the page before each screenshot, e.g. `--click 'The Story: flow only' --tap --wait 24000 --every 1500 index.html`), `--click <text>` (click a link first, e.g. `--click 'The Story' --wait 9000 --every 750 index.html` to watch the story's opening as a series of screenshots), `--every`.
  - `gametest.html` (`src/gametest.ts`, dev only, not built) loads a mission without the menu and runs a scenario in game time (much faster than real time), laying out snapshots and a log: `?scenario=hud|kongs|blonde|kongfall|icons|gameover|bananas|pointer|win` (`gameover&roof=1` waits for a kong to be up on a roof first), `mission=<file>`, `secs=`, `set=kong.climb_chance=1` (any setting, repeatable) and the scenario's own (`blonde&catch=1`, `kongfall&at=climb|top|any&n=3`). `Harness` has `run()` (step the game, with `each`/`until`), `snap()`/`look()` (the world from a free camera), `frame()` (what the player sees, HUD included), `check()` (pass/fail), and casts for the private bits scenarios poke at. Add a scenario to `SCENARIOS` for each new feature worth seeing.
  - `kongtest.html?sheet=...` contact sheets work the same way (`npm run shoot -- 'kongtest.html?model=blonde&sheet=fall'`).

## Architecture (`src/`)

- `main.ts`: menu (`GAMES`: game modes, the story, extras), the pause button/menu (Esc toggles it; the old instant quit is gone) and the story's "Skip to game" link (next mission, or `fly.game` if there is none), then the main loop: `inputmanager.update(); display.beginFrame(); game.play(display, msecs, input)` until status is `QUIT`.
- `core/`: `display.ts` (renderer, 4:3 letterboxed view, offscreen targets; asks for the high-performance GPU and logs the renderer, and the menu warns when the browser is using a software rasteriser), `filmlook.ts` (the old-movie grade: appended to three's `dithering_fragment` chunk so every built-in 3D material gets it after fog; `filmColor()` grades 2D colours that must match, like the FogPlane sky), `draw2d.ts` (immediate-mode 2D batcher in the original's ortho space of 1.0 x 0.75, plus `Drawable2D`/`Quad`/`Image`/`Container2D`/`Text2D` with the original bitmap font metrics, `WordList2D`), `assets.ts` (text, textures, OBJ models, Web Audio, `playBang()`: a synthesised explosion), `settings.ts` (`data/etc/*.properties`), `input.ts` (keyboard/gamepad into an `Input` struct with buttons, axis and a persistent `throttle`; `InputFilter`/`MenuFilter`), `touch.ts` (on-screen stick/throttle/fire, another InputManager device; shown by `main.ts` only while a mission is flying, without the throttle in top-down missions), `PointerTap` in `input.ts` turns canvas taps into `Button.NEXT`; `mtrand.ts` (Mersenne Twister, so a seed always gives the same city).
- `game/`: `component.ts` (`GameComponent` with status `PLAYING`/`NEXT`/`LOST`/`QUIT`; async `load()`, `play()` draws nothing until loaded), `manager.ts` (`GameManager` + `.game` file reader), `stage.ts` (the HTML stage over the 4:3 view: components with `isDom` show a DOM layer each frame with `stage.show()`, unshown layers are dropped at `endFrame()`; `withEffect()` lets transitions fade/clip them), `simple.ts` (Blank and Picture as HTML layers, TitleCard (`cardLayer()`), SoundTrack), `film.ts` (`FilmGrain`: a canvas layer of grain, dust, scratches, flicker and vignette redrawn at 24 fps of real time, with gate weave on an element), `storyteller.ts` (`.shot` cutscenes as an HTML layer: picture plus a dialogue box where consecutive lines stack while they fit, speakers named, a tap/click/Space/Enter moves on), `transition.ts` (fade, spin, starwipe, `hwipe`/`vwipe`/`NNwipe`; with an HTML side the mask is a CSS clip-path, or an inverted mask on the HTML prev over a WebGL mission; between two WebGL components the next is rendered offscreen and drawn through the mask).
- `mission/`: `mission.ts` (Mission + MissionBuilder from `data/missions/*.xml` via `DOMParser`; `<startconditions>` can hold `<kongs respawn_msecs="N|never" health_min="N" health_max="N" height="N" climb_chance="N" on="rooftops" tower_min="N">count</kongs>` (`tower_min`: rooftop kongs on any roof that tall, crowns and spires included, instead of only the tops of the tallest towers), `<blondes grab_chance="N" max="N">on|off</blondes>`, `<bananas>` and `<patrolradius>` (`none`: no edge, the kongs' area moves with the plane; `<roamradius>` sets its size), `<radar>off</radar>`, `<view>topdown</view>` (follow otherwise; there's no switching) and `<controls>steer</controls>` (1942 otherwise); an outcome calls `finish()`, which shows its message (`{stat_name}` is replaced by the stat) for a few seconds with the plane `charmed`, then takes the status; `lastResult` is what the menu shows), `outcomes.ts` (Scenario/Condition/Result, Constraints), `stats.ts` (global `gamestats`), `world.ts` (World scene, Entity/Plane/Projectile, FollowPlaneFilter and TopDownPlaneFilter, Player with its two modes, set by the mission (the propeller is split off the plane model by connected parts ahead of the nose and spun with the throttle); stick input sets smoothed turn rates, yaw is about world up and banking is visual only; CameraMan with a chase camera that blends into the top-down view; `World.update` sweeps each entity with a `radius` from its old to its new position through `CityGrid.sweep()` and against `shootable` entities' `intersect()`, and calls its `hit()` with the target, if any (bullets call the target's `struck()`; bombs also `World.blast()`); top-down bullets are `flat` (no city collisions; `Projectile.update` tests their ground track against shootable entities' `aim()` and curves down to the first ahead); the plane crashes into kongs as into buildings; top-down mode climbs to `CityGrid.maxHeightNear()` plus a clearance), `city.ts` (`CityGrid`: 8x8 patches of 8x8 blocks kept centred on the camera, generated with `MTRand(42)` and merged into per-patch meshes; new patches are generated one per frame as the plane flies. The 2006 generator still sets every lot's position and size; the styling comes from a separate RNG hashed from each building's position, so the MTRand sequence is unchanged: each lot is split into walk-ups, lofts and taller buildings with storefronts or lobbies, cornices, bulkheads and water tanks; skyscrapers get setbacks and 1910s–30s crowns; lamp posts and hydrants are instanced per quarter-patch; all trees (park and street) are instanced cross trees (two quads at right angles, fixed in the world at a random turn); cornices get no overhang on sides shared with a neighbour and never more than half the 2006 `SPACING`, so neighbouring ledges don't overlap and z-fight. Walls are one mesh per patch textured from `facades.ts`. Level of detail: each patch always has a lite version of its buildings (walls straight to the street, no trim, a point for each metal crown; built by the same code with the detail sent to a `NULL` builder so the random numbers match); the full version (storefronts, lobbies, cornices, water tanks, crowns, street furniture) is built lazily, one patch per frame, within `NEAR_DISTANCE` and dropped again beyond it plus `NEAR_KEEP`. Lamps switch to a low-poly post past `LAMP_DISTANCE`; furniture goes past `FURNITURE_DISTANCE`, trees past `TREE_DISTANCE`, patches past the fog. Collisions: the lite builders note boxes through `Builder.solid()` (walls, blocks, a pyramid as three narrowing boxes), kept per block; `sweep()` slab-tests a segment against them and the street), `kongs.ts` (`Kong`: a task list (walk, step, turn, climb, wait, wall) planned at each street crossing (streets run along multiples of `BLOCKSIZE`); climbs pick a ground-level box from `CityGrid.blockBoxes()` on a corner of the crossing whose street face is clear to the top (kidnappers, and kongs carrying a blonde, want a tall one and go on up the boxes stacked on it, stage by stage); a capsule for hits; shot down in the street it topples away from the shot; up on a building it tumbles (a ballistic fall about its middle, swept through `CityGrid.sweep()`, bouncing off walls and ledges, steered to the middle of the street the wall faces or the nearest one); then it lies there and sinks away. `KongTroop` keeps `count` of them in the patrol area, spawning at crossings away from the plane and each other and respawning after deaths; near the area's edge a kong only takes streets back in), `kongrig.ts` (`Rig`: a skeleton placed by hand on a model in its own units, skin weights from each vertex's distance to the bones, two-bone IK for the arms and legs; `RigAnimator` blends between clips; `KongRig`'s clips are functions of a phase, plus `grab` (reaching in at a window) and, with `carry`, the right arm holding a blonde over the other clips; `KongAnimator` advances walk and climb by distance moved so feet and hands stay planted), `blonde.ts` (`BlondeRig` on `blonde.obj`, clips `stand`/`held`/`fall`/`landed` with the phase in seconds; `Blonde`: held, falling, caught or landed; `BlondeKit`: config, the catching plane, `events` for the mission's messages; synthesised `playScream`/`playChime` in `assets.ts`, like `playGunshot`/`playRicochet`/`playThud` for the guns), `effects.ts` (particles: one `THREE.Points` per layer (sparks, fire, smoke) with per-particle size and RGBA in a ShaderMaterial that does its own fog and gets the film grade; `sparks()`, `explosion()`, `furHit()`, `dust()`), `facades.ts` (procedural canvas textures: a texture array of facade kinds, 4x4 window bays per tile, mapped so each face is a whole number of bays and floors line up; plus the street tile, painted to the 2006 `city_block_base.png` layout), `finale.ts` (`Finale`: the game-over shot; `KongTroop.finaleKong()` picks a kong on a roof or summons one, `Kong.showOff()` keeps it roaring; the close-up side is picked clear by `CityGrid.sweep()`), `seethrough.ts` (patches the city's materials at load with a screen-space hole round a point, cutting what's nearer than it; off unless `setSeeThrough()`), `hud.ts` (FogPlane, CountDownTimer, ClawAlert, LivesDisplay, Radar, TurnBack, KongPointer, IconTally, KissPop, FPS readout, LoadBar).
- `tuner.ts`: lil-gui built from `data/etc/toggles.xml`; Insert or backquote toggles it and pauses the current component.

Conventions: textures use `flipY = false` so texcoord v=0 is the top of the image (the models' and 2D drawing's texcoords assume it), and `Picture`/`Blank` default to status `NEXT`, so they show for one frame unless a transition is drawing them. `play()` gets `input = null` when a component is drawn frozen (paused, or by a transition). Some 2006 quirks are still in place and commented (e.g. the constraint keys in `mission.ts`); they can be fixed now rather than preserved.

## Game data (`data/`)

- `.game` files: one component per line: `storyteller <file.shot>`, `mission <file.xml>`, `blank <hexcolor>`, `picture <img> [msecs] [hexcolor] [film]` (`film`: old-film grain over it), `card <msecs> <text>` (a title card, `|` breaks lines; stays up until the mission after it has generated its city, which the manager loads early), `music <file>`, and `transition <type> <msecs>`, which links the previous and next components. `#` comments.
- `etc/`: config (`display`, `story`, `physics`, `game`, `main`, `tuner`, `city`, `kong`, `blonde` properties; `toggles.xml`; `projectiles/`), `missions/` XML, `story/` `.shot` scripts and images, `models/` (`.obj`), `images/`, `sound/`.
